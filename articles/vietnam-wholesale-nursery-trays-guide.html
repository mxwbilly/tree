Vietnam Importer's Guide to Wholesale Nursery Trays from China

June 2026  ·  Vietnam market  ·  Nursery & propagation

Vietnam's nursery and horticulture sector has grown steadily over the past five years, with flower exporters, seedling distributors, and retail garden centers all expanding their sourcing volumes. Wholesale nursery trays from China remain the most cost-efficient option for Vietnamese importers — but finding a reliable supplier, negotiating the right MOQ, and managing the logistics into Ho Chi Minh City or Hanoi requires a clear process.

This guide covers everything a Vietnamese importer needs to know before placing their first or next container of nursery pots and propagation trays from China: product specs, pricing benchmarks, minimum order quantities, shipping routes, customs duties under RCEP, and the supplier checklist that separates reliable factories from risky ones.

Why Vietnam Importers Choose Chinese Nursery Trays

China produces the majority of the world's plastic nursery trays and seedling propagation flats. The combination of vertically integrated plastic processing, low tooling costs, and competitive ocean freight to Vietnamese ports — primarily Cat Lai (Ho Chi Minh City) and Hai Phong — makes China the default sourcing destination for Vietnamese buyers at all order sizes.

Key advantages for Vietnamese buyers

  • RCEP tariff reduction — under RCEP, import duties on plastic horticultural products between China and Vietnam have been progressively reduced. Many nursery tray HS codes now qualify for 0–5% preferential duty rates versus the standard MFN rate.
  • Short ocean transit — FOB Shenzhen or Guangzhou to Cat Lai port is typically 5–8 days. Ho Chi Minh City-based importers can plan tight replenishment cycles.
  • Stackable design = lower freight cost — well-designed stackable trays nest tightly, significantly reducing the cubic metre (CBM) per unit and lowering ocean freight cost per piece.
  • Flexible MOQ — most Chinese factories accept LCL orders from 500 pieces for standard tray sizes, making it viable for importers who are testing a new cell configuration before committing to an FCL.

Nursery Tray Product Specifications to Confirm Before Ordering

Not all nursery trays are the same. Vietnamese buyers — especially those supplying vegetable seedling distributors or flower exporters — need to confirm the following specs before requesting a quotation:

Specification Standard range Notes for Vietnam market
Cell count per tray 32 / 50 / 72 / 105 / 128 cells 72-cell most popular for vegetable seedlings; 32-cell for flower plugs
Tray dimensions 540 × 280 mm (standard); 540 × 540 mm (large) Confirm bench width at end buyer's nursery
Material PS (polystyrene) or PP (polypropylene) PP is more durable for multi-cycle use; PS is lower cost for single use
Wall thickness 0.5 mm – 1.2 mm Thicker walls = more reuse cycles; specify if selling to commercial growers
Drainage holes 1–3 holes per cell Required for most propagation applications; confirm with end buyer
Stack height reduction 60–75% when stacked Critical for CBM calculation; request stacked dimension from supplier
Color Black, grey, white, green Black absorbs heat — less suitable for exposed outdoor use in southern Vietnam

Pro tip: Always request the stacked dimensions (L × W × H per 100 trays) before confirming price. A poorly designed tray that only compresses 40% will double your freight cost compared to a tray that compresses 70%.

RCEP Duty Rates for Nursery Trays Imported into Vietnam

Under the RCEP agreement (effective between China and Vietnam since January 2022), plastic horticultural products classified under HS 3923.10 (boxes, cases, crates and similar articles) or HS 3926.90 (other plastic articles) qualify for preferential duty rates when accompanied by a Form E Certificate of Origin issued by a Chinese authorized body.

  • Standard MFN duty: 10–15%
  • RCEP preferential rate (2026): 3–5% depending on specific HS code
  • Required document: Form E CO (from CCPIT or local Chinese customs authority)
  • VAT on import: 10% (standard Vietnamese import VAT)

Always request Form E from your Chinese supplier before goods depart. It cannot be issued retroactively once the shipment has left China. Most established suppliers can arrange this within 3–5 working days of booking.

Shipping Routes: China to Vietnam

Port options for Vietnamese importers

  • Cat Lai (Ho Chi Minh City) — most common entry point for southern Vietnam buyers. Transit from Shenzhen/Guangzhou: 5–8 days.
  • Hai Phong — preferred for Hanoi and northern Vietnam distribution. Transit from Shenzhen: 7–10 days; from Tianjin or Qingdao: 4–6 days.
  • Da Nang — less common for nursery trays but used by central Vietnam buyers to reduce inland trucking cost.

Typical lead times from China

  • Sample order (courier): 5–7 working days to Ho Chi Minh City
  • LCL bulk shipment: 25–35 days from order confirmation to port clearance
  • FCL bulk shipment: 20–30 days from order confirmation to port clearance

Seasonal note: Chinese Lunar New Year (January–February) causes a 2–4 week factory shutdown. Vietnamese importers planning spring season stock should place orders no later than mid-December to avoid delays.

How to Qualify a Chinese Nursery Tray Supplier

The biggest risk for first-time importers is receiving trays that look correct in photos but fail in the field — warping under UV exposure, cracking on the first propagation cycle, or dimensions that don't match the stated spec sheet. The following checklist reduces this risk significantly.

Supplier qualification checklist

  1. Request physical samples before bulk order — pay for samples and evaluate actual wall thickness, drainage hole quality, and stackability in your own facility.
  2. Ask for business license and export license — legitimate factories will provide these without hesitation. A trading company without factory access creates an extra layer of quality risk.
  3. Confirm production capacity and lead time in writing — peak season (March–June) can push lead times 2–3 weeks longer than off-peak.
  4. Request packing list and carton specifications — understand how many trays per carton, carton weight, and CBM per carton before calculating freight cost.
  5. Verify Form E availability — not all Chinese factories are registered RCEP Form E issuers. Confirm before committing to an order if RCEP duty savings are part of your cost model.
  6. Check reorder flexibility — good suppliers will accommodate partial repeat orders (e.g., 1,000 pcs of a 72-cell tray and 500 pcs of a 50-cell tray in the same LCL shipment).

For importers serving multiple segments, a mixed LCL shipment combining two or three tray configurations is typically the most efficient way to start — it tests market demand before committing to FCL quantities in a single SKU.
